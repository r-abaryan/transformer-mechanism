## Before training

- Read the [research checkpoint](docs/assessments/research-checkpoint.md) and [failure register](docs/assessments/failure-register.md). Avoid repeating a closed study without a distinct question.
- Freeze the task, exact labels, split policy, seeds, budget, metrics and stopping rule before evaluation. Include per-seed and per-group results and simple baselines.
- Plan final training-pool, validation and test accuracy/loss together. For language prediction use the relevant token loss/perplexity. If full training evaluation is impractical, freeze a representative training subset and disclose that limit.
- Measure held-out performance at training lengths separately from longer-length transfer. Include controls for shortcuts, such as late resets erasing earlier history.
- When resets make earlier bits irrelevant, check that initial and reset values vary independently. Complementary label balancing can leave those cues perfectly correlated; include disagreeing-value counterfactuals.
- Check labels independently, verify relevant outputs/gradients and include an intentional error control. These checks support correctness within their scope; they do not prove every implementation detail correct.
- Derive Cartesian diagnostic counts from the lengths of the declared factors before evaluation. Check the design's count independently; a wrong final count can discard an otherwise completed probe run. See the [source-marker count correction](docs/protocols/copy-source-end-marker-count-correction.md).
- Before training, exercise final evaluator and independent audit entry on supplied logits and an initialized saved checkpoint. Include absent groups and reload/import paths. A helper verified on balanced groups may still reject a valid missing group; a compiled audit may still fail at runtime. See the [EOS evaluation recovery](docs/topics/benchmarks/copy-eos-training-results.md#evaluation-defects-recovery-and-verification).

## Diagnose failures before proposing causes

| Evidence | Permitted conclusion |
|---|---|
| High training fit, low held-out performance | A generalization gap exists; its cause remains open |
| Low training and held-out performance | The task was not mastered under this setup; optimization, capacity, supervision and exposure remain candidate explanations |
| Short held-out success, longer-input failure | Length transfer fails within the tested conditions |
| Weak groups or seeds hidden by a good mean | Reliability fails even if the aggregate improves |

Never infer architectural impossibility, convergence, memorization or a learned mechanism solely from task accuracy. Distinguish an implementation defect, a failed empirical criterion and a missing measurement. Missing diagnostics limit interpretation; they do not automatically invalidate verified runs.

## Follow-up discipline

- A closed run ends its declared budget; it does not settle the topic. Prefer a distinct explanatory question tied to observed behavior over filling a coverage gap. Current focus and history belong in the research checkpoint.
- For future comparisons, separate bounded recipe development from confirmation. Declare candidate settings and selection metrics first, record every attempt, establish full training-group fit, then lock the recipe and reserve fresh confirmation. Validation selection is allowed when declared; repeatedly selecting on confirmation results is not. Preserve earlier fixed-budget failures.

- First inspect existing checkpoints when that can answer the question without training. Preserve original sources, protocols, reports and checkpoints; store diagnostic additions separately.
- For a new training comparison use matched initial values, data streams and declared budgets where applicable. Historical scores are reference points, not paired controls.
- Changing supervision changes the objective and amount of target feedback. Report loss weighting, supervised positions and compute; do not call it an architecture change or silently assume equal learning cost.
- Record observations, interpretations and unknowns separately. Independent numerical checks verify arithmetic, not broad generalization. Prediction counts and repeated timings are not independent training seeds.
- End at the predefined stopping rule. A failed criterion does not automatically authorize tuning, longer training or replacement seeds. Record failures and preserve valid earlier evidence.
